## CP01 — Compare two accounts of the work

**Purpose:** learn how to find the actual bottleneck while respecting the people doing the work.

**Original fictional case seed:** a local transport-office owner wants faster replies because they suspect lost enquiries. The coordinator says the delay often comes from missing journey details and waiting for someone to confirm vehicle suitability. Neither statement has yet been verified by a run of recent cases.

**Practice:** write the goal, each person's account, what a supplied example actually shows, and what remains unknown. Ask the owner about the consequence and the coordinator to walk through a recent task. Use a permissioned demonstration, fictional transcript or written walkthrough. Recording is optional; it requires agreement when used. Preserve the distinction between a report, an observation and an assumption.

**Saved result:** a short evidence table and three neutral questions, followed by a small map of the trigger, steps, waits, handoffs and completed outcome. A useful question might ask which detail was missing in the latest delayed enquiry and what happened next.

**Review criteria for later authoring:** questions seek examples without assuming AI is the answer; the business goal remains visible; disagreement leads to investigation rather than deciding that the manager or employee must be wrong. Scale interview depth to the question being resolved instead of imposing a fixed count or 45-minute minimum.

**Changed case:** a fresh example shows the reply was prompt but the required vehicle was unavailable. Reconsider the proposed bottleneck and identify what evidence would distinguish lost demand from an actual capacity limit.

## CP02 — Choose and validate one opportunity

**Purpose:** make a provisional recommendation and improve it with client knowledge.

**Practice:** compare three plausible improvements from the selected project. Record expected impact, total delivery and support effort, evidence confidence, access/dependencies, consequences of errors and the person who will use it. Include a simpler process change or no change where appropriate. An impact–effort matrix is an optional view of the same list; no drag-only interface is needed.

Then ask the owner and operator which option addresses the important problem, what hidden steps were missed, and what would make the proposed routine difficult to adopt. Mark each input as confirmed, disputed or untested. Keep unresolved feasibility questions visible for a technical partner or small test.

**Saved result:** a before-and-after ranking, the reason for any change, the chosen pilot, its user and measurable outcome. Client agreement is recorded separately from technical verification.

**Review criteria for later authoring:** the preferred option follows evidence; uncertainty is not hidden in a numerical score; a client can disagree without being treated as an obstacle. A chatbot, dashboard or email sorter is not automatically a quick win just because it belongs to that category.

**Changed case:** the initially preferred option depends on a system that cannot provide the required data in the proposed way. Revise the scope or ranking and state the smallest remaining feasibility check. Do not promise a fixed 30–90-day result before that work is understood.

## CP03 — Explain value without inflating it

**Purpose:** connect improvements to benefits the client can actually realise.

**Practice:** keep four separate parts:

1. **Capacity:** case volume, baseline checked handling time, proposed handling time and the share of cases the workflow can handle. Include review and correction time.
2. **Cash costs avoided:** specify the bill, paid overtime, contractor work or other expenditure that would actually fall. Record whether that change is observed or proposed.
3. **Additional contribution:** explain what extra work could be sold and delivered, with demand, conversion, capacity and associated costs visible. Extra sales revenue is not automatically profit.
4. **Costs and period:** include setup, tools, usage, supervision, correction, training and maintenance as applicable; state the measurement period and when benefits begin.

**Original fictional calculation seed:** 200 comparable enquiries per month, an observed eight-minute average baseline and a proposed five-minute checked process imply ten hours of potential monthly capacity if every case qualifies. At 80% coverage, the estimate is eight hours. Salaries do not automatically decrease. The five-minute target and 80% coverage remain assumptions until tested.

**Separate fictional financial example:** assume the client can genuinely avoid NOK 24,000 of expenditure over a full first operating year, with NOK 12,000 setup and NOK 6,000 continuing costs. Total year-one cost is NOK 18,000; net benefit is NOK 6,000; the simple ROI is 33.3%. These are invented amounts for arithmetic, not service prices, wage benchmarks or a projection for the enquiry case above.

`Simple ROI = (financial benefits − total costs) / total costs × 100%`

**Saved result:** a short value worksheet showing evidence labels, assumptions, a conservative and a central scenario, and one next measurement. A more optimistic scenario is optional. If the financial benefit is unestablished, retain the capacity and service-quality case without inventing ROI. With zero costs, this ratio is undefined rather than infinite.

**Review criteria for later authoring:** no double-counted time; matching units and periods; costs have an explicit scope; estimated percentages do not gain authority through extra decimal places; negative results remain visible. The calculation is arithmetic assistance, not automatic validation of the assumptions.

**Changed case:** handling time falls but the business cannot use the released hours and its expenditure is unchanged. Reassess the benefit, service-quality evidence and proposed fee rather than inventing additional sales.

**Optional later variant:** distinguish outreach stages—messages, replies, qualified conversations, proposals, signed clients and paid work. Ask for the denominator and period before accepting a conversion claim. This is interpretation practice, not an automated outreach feature.

## CP04 — Help the client make one decision

**Purpose:** explain the evidence and a proportionate next step clearly.

**Practice:** prepare five short sections: problem and scope; what was learned; available options; recommended pilot; expected benefits, costs and next decision. Include the uncertainty that would change the recommendation. Use the client's everyday language and actual process examples.

**Saved result:** a one-page decision brief or a short spoken explanation with notes. Slides are optional. Keep a projected business case separate from measured pilot results.

**Review criteria for later authoring:** the client can identify the recommendation, its reason, their contribution and the decision being requested. The brief remains useful if the client declines the proposed implementation. It does not imply that buying an audit commits them to a build.

**Changed case:** the owner asks for a guaranteed revenue increase. Explain what the evidence supports, how the pilot will test the assumptions and what you can commit to delivering.

## CP05 — Define the assessment and delivery partnership

**Purpose:** understand how to sell useful investigation and coordinate implementation within demonstrated capability.

**Practice:** draft an assessment for one defined workflow. Specify the question, included interviews or examples, deliverable, scope limit and decision the client will be able to make. An assessment can recommend a process change, a pilot, further investigation or no build. Price and duration depend on agreed scope and value; the source's dollar minimums are not course defaults.

Then decide which work Martin can perform, which needs a specialist, and how the specialist's capability would be checked. Request an inspectable comparable example or a bounded trial with defined acceptance evidence. Write down who confirms feasibility before commitments, communicates with the client, builds, checks the result, fixes faults, maintains the system and owns accounts and deliverables. Disclose a relevant commercial relationship when recommending a delivery partner.

**Saved result:** a bounded assessment offer and partner handover note, linked to L06's existing operator, cost, support and exit questions. No actual partner is selected or contacted by this activity.

**Review criteria for later authoring:** the offer gives useful information independently of an implementation sale; responsibility does not disappear into the word outsourced; capability claims are supported; maintenance is scoped rather than sold as guaranteed success. Practising discovery does not require the ability to code every eventual solution.

**Changed case:** the proposed partner can demonstrate a build but cannot support it after delivery. Revise the operating arrangement, find a viable support route or narrow the offer before promising the client a maintained service.

## Optional demonstration variants for later L04/L05 practice

- **Request received or booking confirmed?** Supply an original fictional voice conversation and separate backend evidence. Ask what may honestly be said, which current request and capacity were checked, and whether a booking record exists. A friendly confirmation alone is insufficient; a missing trace is uncertainty rather than proof the real video failed. [AD1, T6, T7]
- **Which policy is current?** Supply an old retrieved passage and an approved newer rule. Ask which governs the response, what needs updating, and when a person should resolve the conflict. Retrieval relevance does not establish current authority. [AD1, T8]

These are optional future case briefs. They do not alter the current L04/L05 content versions or imply a working voice assistant, retrieval service or customer connection in the app.
